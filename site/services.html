01 / Travel

Meet the traveller in the right moment.

Start with the traveller’s situation. Clarify the product or experience’s useful role. A destination backdrop alone does not explain creator fit.

Client evidence / Holafly

Always-on creator collaboration.

Continuous sourcing, advance planning, refreshed briefs and repeat collaboration connected the programme.

Inspect Holafly’s programme process

Build the selection around relevance.

Holafly’s selection included comedy, family, sport and interviews alongside travel. Audience relevance and the market brief guided the choice.

  • Audience: which traveller or market matters? Why is this creator relevant?
  • Product role: which recognisable problem or experience should the story show?
  • Creative context: how can the creator use their own format?

Monthly briefs followed problem → emotion → product → resolution. The structure was adapted to each creator and audience, not treated as a guaranteed formula.

Keep geography specific to the brief.

For a US brief, choose a national or location priority. New York, Los Angeles, Miami, Chicago, Austin or San Francisco may matter. Keep a location when it changes the audience, setting, timing or coverage.

Locations are brief inputs, not claims of offices, past local results or guaranteed delivery.

Planning reference / Audience, journey and measurement

Define the journey before the geography.

Which traveller matters: US residents going abroad, overseas visitors considering the US, or domestic travellers? Define the audience and decision. Match the creator’s audience evidence to that group.

The US National Travel and Tourism Office’s SIAT survey separates inbound and outbound international air travellers. It records trip purpose, destinations, booking methods and advance planning. It does not cover all domestic trips.

Read the NTTO survey’s scope and planning fields.

What should a useful travel brief contain?
  • Commercial question: explain the product, support consideration, or learn which creator interpretation fits?
  • Traveller context: audience, origin/destination relevance, journey stage and supporting audience evidence.
  • Creative truth: approved product facts, evidence for benefit claims and room for the creator’s experience.
  • Delivery checkpoints: agreed brief, creator rationale, feedback owner, approval decision and coverage record.

Resolve the objective at Frame. Choose creators at Assemble. Revisit the brief when the market or message changes.

How should traffic and campaign outcomes be compared?

If the brand uses Google Analytics, agree link names before publication. UTM parameters identify referring campaigns; values are case-sensitive. Confirm the destination, campaign identifier and creative label. Test the link.

Google’s campaign URL guidance.

Separate content views, tagged traffic and business actions. Confirm the data owner and reporting window. Can a sale or enquiry be linked to a measured visit? Agree tracking per brief; this reference does not establish an IRG integration.

02 / Hospitality

An experience worth telling. A brief worth agreeing.

A stay, route or seasonal experience needs creative and practical planning. Define the audience, distinctive setting and requirements before shortlisting.

Illustrative methodology / No client results claimed

A hospitality travel brief.

The format shows a shortlist, brief and stay-coverage structure. It is a planning example, not a named client campaign.

Inspect the hospitality planning example

This format does not establish booking or hosted-stay results. Holafly’s travel-brand case is not hospitality booking proof.

Connect the creative and the practical requirements.

  • Audience and occasion: who should care about the stay, route or season?
  • Experience: what must be understood about the property or setting? Where can the creator interpret?
  • Arrangements: agree dates, access, inclusions, exclusions and on-site responsibilities.
  • Coverage and approval: which outputs are required? Who consolidates feedback and approves changes?
  • Rights and disclosure: confirm proposed uses, permissions and sponsorship requirements.

Resolve these questions for the engagement. Terms, delivery timing and compliance requirements need agreement.

Choose the measurement question before the stay.

Agree the available content signals and business data. Confirm their owner and limits. Views and engagement alone do not establish booking impact.

A location belongs in the operating plan.

For a US property or several locations, agree access, the on-site owner and coverage for each. Include a city only when it changes the work.

Planning reference / Experience, permissions and booking evidence

Make the stay workable before promising the coverage.

Separate the experience to communicate from the arrangements needed to capture it. Clear facts and practical limits give the creator room to interpret.

What needs agreement before arrival?
  • Before the stay: dates, travelling party, access, included/excluded costs, property contact and required facts.
  • During the stay: filming permissions, guest/staff privacy, unavailable spaces and the owner of changed requirements.
  • Before publication: outputs, factual checks, consolidated feedback, approval owner and agreed content use.
  • After delivery: content links, delivery record, available reporting and questions for the next activity.

Define reuse: brand channels, paid activity or another use. Agree channels, duration and permissions. A hosted stay does not grant these rights automatically.

How should a hosted relationship be disclosed?

FTC guidance includes free or discounted services and perks as relationships requiring disclosure in US-facing endorsements. Put a clear disclosure with the endorsement, not on a profile or behind “more”.

FTC disclosure guidance.

Identify the relationship, content format, review owner and applicable requirements. Agree disclosure and permissions alongside creative approval.

Keep a booking question connected to booking data.

Can the brand identify a completed booking? Confirm its source system, period and attribution settings. Views, clicks and bookings are different observations. State the limit if only views and clicks are available.

Google defines attribution as assigning credit to touchpoints before an important action. State the chosen model in the report. Agree the brand’s reporting access and setup.

Google’s attribution overview.

Context before volume. A reason for the next cycle.

Connect creator, audience and experience. Explain the brand fit, the creator’s interpretation and the campaign objective.

Define the relationship around the brief.

  • Audience context: what matters to the audience? Why does this creator fit?
  • Brand suitability: which tone, setting and content fit the relationship?
  • Creative opportunity: what should the creator communicate? Where can they interpret?
  • Continuity: which creator rationale, decisions and open questions should inform this cycle?

Decide what should carry forward.

Connect selection, briefing, delivery and review. Ongoing work adds retained context and a recurring rhythm. Agree cadence and scope for the engagement; no fixed timetable or uplift is assumed.

For a national US audience, include locations when they change audience, creative or delivery needs. Location detail does not imply an office or guaranteed coverage.

Planning reference / Creator experience and retained learning

Start with the situation the audience recognises.

Choose a routine, occasion, interest or use case. Why is the brand relevant? How can the creator demonstrate it in their own voice? Focus on fit rather than a longer shortlist.

Which questions make creator fit inspectable?
  • Audience relevance: what evidence connects the creator’s audience to the intended customer or question?
  • Creative fit: which existing formats suit this use case?
  • Brand suitability: review tone, content context and previous brand work.
  • Practical fit: confirm information, product experience and approval dependencies.
  • Repeat potential: what must we learn before considering another brief?
What needs checking in a product story?

FTC guidance calls for genuine creator experience and evidence for product claims. Describe the brand relationship clearly.

FTC guidance on endorsements and product claims.

Ask the brand for permitted claims, supporting evidence and facts to review. Agree disclosure and approval responsibilities.

Record what changes in the next cycle.

Record the objective, creator/format signals, business context, reporting limits and next recommendation. An unusually strong piece of content does not establish a programme-wide conclusion.

Google’s content parameter distinguishes creatives when different links are used. Agree consistent names and the reporting owner before comparing results. Keep definitions stable so each cycle can ask a useful new question.

Google’s campaign-tagging reference.
