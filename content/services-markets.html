Build the selection around relevance.

In the Holafly case, creator selection broadened beyond travel into comedy, family, sport and interviews, reaching nine niches by June. The selection followed audience relevance and market targets.

  • Audience: which traveller or market needs to care, and what makes this creator relevant to them?
  • Product role: which recognisable problem or experience belongs in the story?
  • Creative context: how can the creator interpret the idea in their own format?

The case’s monthly briefs used problem → emotion → product → resolution, adapted to the creator and audience. This explains the approach used in that programme; it is not a guaranteed creative formula.

Keep geography specific to the brief.

For a US-wide brief, state whether the priority is a national audience or particular locations such as New York, Los Angeles, Miami, Chicago, Austin or San Francisco. Ask which locations actually change the audience, creative setting, timing or required coverage. These are planning inputs, not claims of offices or past client work in those cities.

The supplied US market evidence is California: 3.78M organic views in August 2026, on the view target and 9% below planned cost. The full programme figure covers all four reported markets and must not be presented as a US-only total.

Planning reference / Audience, journey and measurement

Define the journey before the geography.

A US-focused brief should distinguish US residents travelling abroad, overseas visitors considering the US and people making domestic trips. Ask which group matters, which decision the content should support and whether the creator’s audience evidence matches that group.

The US National Travel and Tourism Office’s SIAT research separates inbound and outbound international air travellers and records trip purpose, destinations, booking methods and advance planning. Its scope is international air travel; it is not a census of all domestic trips.

Read the NTTO survey’s scope and planning fields.

What should a useful travel brief contain?
  • Commercial question: are we trying to explain a product, support consideration or learn which creator interpretation is relevant?
  • Traveller context: intended audience, origin/destination relevance, journey stage and available audience evidence.
  • Creative truth: approved product information, substantiation for any benefit claim and room for the creator’s own experience.
  • Delivery checkpoints: the agreed brief, selection rationale, brand feedback owner, approval decision and final coverage record.

Use the Frame stage to resolve the objective, then make the creator decision at Assemble. Revisit the brief if the market or message changes.

How should traffic and campaign outcomes be compared?

If the brand uses Google Analytics, agree campaign-link naming before publication. Google documents how UTM parameters identify referring campaigns and notes that their values are case-sensitive. Agree the destination, campaign identifier and creative label, then test the actual link.

Ask who supplies each figure, which reporting window applies and whether a sale or enquiry can be connected to a measured visit. 02 / Hospitality

An experience worth telling. A brief worth agreeing.

A stay, route or seasonal experience connects creative interpretation with practical delivery. Define who should find the experience relevant, what makes the setting distinctive and what needs agreement before shortlisting.

Connect the creative and the practical requirements.

  • Audience and occasion: who should find the stay, route or season relevant?
  • Experience: what needs to be understood about the property or setting, and where can the creator interpret?
  • Arrangements: which dates, access, inclusions, exclusions and on-site responsibilities need agreement?
  • Coverage and approval: which outputs are required, who consolidates feedback and who can approve a change?
  • Rights and disclosure: which proposed uses, permissions and sponsorship requirements must be confirmed?

These are questions to resolve for the actual engagement. They do not establish standard contract terms, fixed delivery timing or universal compliance assurances.

Choose the measurement question before the stay.

Agree which content signals or business data will be available, who supplies them and what the reporting can support. Views and engagement do not automatically establish booking impact.

A location belongs in the operating plan.

If the brief involves a US property or several locations, clarify access, the person responsible on site and the coverage required at each location. Avoid adding cities that do not change the work.

Planning reference / Experience, permissions and booking evidence

Make the stay workable before promising the coverage.

The brief should distinguish an experience to communicate from the operational arrangements needed to capture it. A creator can interpret the experience more credibly when the required information and practical limits are clear.

What needs agreement before arrival?
  • Before the stay: dates, travelling party, access, included/excluded costs, the property contact and required factual information.
  • During the stay: what can be filmed, any guest/staff privacy concerns, any unavailable spaces and who can resolve a changed requirement.
  • Before publication: outputs, factual checks, consolidated feedback, approval ownership and the agreed use of the content.
  • After delivery: content links, the delivery record, available reporting and the questions the next activity should answer.

Confirm whether reuse means a brand’s own channels, paid activity or another use, then agree the channels, duration and permissions. These are procurement questions, not pre-set rights granted by a hosted stay.

How should a hosted relationship be disclosed?

FTC guidance for US-facing endorsements includes free or discounted services and other perks among relationships that require disclosure. It advises placing a clear disclosure with the endorsement itself rather than hiding it on a profile page or behind a “more” interaction.

FTC disclosure guidance.

For the actual brief, identify the relationship, proposed content format, review owner and requirements that apply. Approval of creative does not replace agreement on disclosure or content permissions.

Keep a booking question connected to booking data.

Before judging commercial impact, ask whether the brand can identify a completed booking, which source system records it and which period and attribution settings apply. A content view, a link click and a booking are different observations. If only the first two are available, state that limit.

Google defines attribution as assigning credit to touchpoints on the path to an important action. That makes the chosen model part of the reporting definition, rather than a number to leave unexplained. A brand’s actual reporting access and setup still need agreement.

03 / Lifestyle

Context before volume. A reason for the next cycle.

Start with the connection between creator, audience and experience. A relevant recommendation explains why the brand relationship makes sense, how the creator could interpret it and what the campaign needs to achieve.

Define the relationship around the brief.

  • Audience context: what matters to this audience, and why is this creator a credible fit?
  • Brand suitability: which tone, setting and content support the proposed relationship?
  • Creative opportunity: what should the creator communicate, and where is there room for their own interpretation?
  • Continuity: what previous creator rationale, campaign decisions or open questions should inform this cycle?

Decide what should carry forward.

A campaign activation needs a connected path through selection, briefing, delivery and review. An ongoing programme adds retained context and a recurring planning rhythm. Agree the cadence and scope around the actual work; no universal timetable or uplift is promised.

For a national US audience, decide whether geographic detail changes the brief. Specific locations should be included because they change an audience question, creative requirement or delivery dependency, not to imply a local office or guaranteed coverage.

Planning reference / Creator experience and retained learning

Start with the situation the audience recognises.

A lifestyle brief can focus on a routine, occasion, interest or use case. Ask which context makes the brand relevant and how the creator can demonstrate it without losing their own voice. Keep this separate from simply expanding the number of names on the shortlist.

Which questions make creator fit inspectable?
  • Audience relevance: what evidence connects the creator’s audience with the intended customer or question?
  • Creative fit: which existing formats show a credible way to interpret this specific use case?
  • Brand suitability: which tone, content context and previous brand work should the team review?
  • Practical fit: what information, experience with the product and approval dependencies are needed?
  • Repeat potential: what would we need to learn before deciding whether another brief with this creator is useful?
What needs checking in a product story?

The FTC’s guidance says endorsements should reflect the creator’s real experience and should not invent product claims requiring evidence the advertiser lacks. It also advises clear language for the brand relationship.

FTC guidance on endorsements and product claims.

Ask the brand to identify the claims the creator may make, the evidence behind them and any information that needs a factual review. Agree the disclosure and approval responsibilities for the proposed activity.

Record what changes in the next cycle.

A useful learning note contains the objective, observed creator/format signals, available business context, reporting limitations and the next recommendation. Distinguish a single unusually strong piece of content from a conclusion that should apply to the whole programme.

If different creative links are used, Google’s campaign guidance includes a content parameter for distinguishing creatives. Agree consistent names and the reporting owner before comparing results. A recurring programme should preserve the definitions so each review asks a useful new question.
